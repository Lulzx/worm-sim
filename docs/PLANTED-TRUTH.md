# Planted-truth controls for the capacity gate

The [capacity gate](LEVEL0-CAPACITY-PROTOCOL.md) asks whether Level 0 can capture
90% of the zero-start mean-response energy on the ADAL/ADAR training targets.
Every optimizer and numerics variant so far has stopped between 74.8% and
79.31%, and the protocol states that a failed gate alone does not identify its
cause. This document adds the missing controls: targets generated by a known
model, fitted with the unchanged harness. Everything below uses training trials
only. No validation or test response is read.

Tool: [`backends/jax/planted_truth.py`](../backends/jax/planted_truth.py)
(`noise`, `make`, `score`). Synthetic exports carry a `synthetic` block, and
`fit.py` rejects them, so they cannot enter a benchmark fit. `overfit.py`
records the block in its manifest.

## Step 1: noise ceiling of the real targets (measured)

A training target is a confidence-weighted mean over trials from different
animals. Its sampling error is part of the energy the gate counts as
explainable, yet no deterministic shared-response model can reproduce it. The
`noise` command estimates that error per neuron and sample from the trials,
using the unbiased weighted variance of the mean. Neurons seen in only one trial
take the target's pooled variance.

| Quantity | Value |
| --- | ---: |
| Zero-response MSE (matches the protocol) | 0.05145506 |
| Zero-start mean-response bound (matches the protocol) | 0.04153675 |
| Explainable energy | 0.00991831 |
| Sampling-noise energy | 0.00446613 |
| Noise share | **45.0%** |
| Expected capture of a correct model | **55.0%** |

[Receipt](planted-truth-noise-ceiling.json). An earlier scratch estimate used a
random split-half of the same trials (200 splits) and gave 47–48%. That method
and this one agree to within the difference in how they treat single-trial neurons.

**Consequence.** On these two targets, a model that reproduced the true shared
response exactly would capture about 55%, not 100%. The 90% gate can be passed
only by fitting trial sampling noise. The real fits' 74.8–79.31% therefore
already exceed the expected capture of a correct model. That is consistent
with the fit absorbing noise through about 7,600 trainable parameters and 302
per-neuron gains, against 374 traces of 40 samples. Step 2 tests this reading
with a known truth instead of a noise model.

## Step 2: planted-truth fits (declared before any run)

**Truth.** `runs/level0-capacity-adal-adar-1000-prep120/epoch-950.json`, a
Level 0 member fitted to the real targets (76.48% real capture). It has the
same parameter layout, observation configuration, 0.01 s Euler step and 120 s
preparation as the fits below. Its synthetic signal energy is 0.00753, against
0.00992 real explainable energy.

**Fits.** Each arm runs `overfit.py` with the reference run's exact settings:
- seed-1 epoch-zero model, rest −0.2, per-neuron gains starting at 10;
- Adam at 0.01 for 1,000 updates, dt 0.01, 120 s preparation;
- targets ADAL and ADAR.

| Arm | Planted targets | Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **B**: clean, in class | Truth response, no noise | Can the optimizer reach a known in-class optimum at this budget? |
| **A**: realistic, in class | Truth response plus real trial residuals: per neuron, the random-sign weighted mean of its own trial residuals, variance-corrected to the sampling variance of the real mean. Seed 1 | How much noise does the fit absorb, and how well does it recover the signal? |
| **C**: clean, out of class | Truth with short-term depression on all 3,638 chemical edges ([spec](../configs/planted-truth-depression.json)), energy-matched to B | Does the gate react to a mechanism Level 0 lacks? |

The depression settings for arm C were chosen from truth-only properties
before any fit. The first setting tried removed 94% of the signal energy, which
amounts to a near-uncoupled network. The chosen setting is the mildest of the five
tried. A single gain change cannot reproduce 37% of its response shape.

**Metrics** (`score`, against the noise-free planted signal):
- final and best training capture, as `overfit.py` reports it;
- signal recovery, 1 − Σw(prediction − signal)² / Σw·signal²;
- noise absorbed, Σw(prediction − signal)(target − signal) / Σw(target − signal)²;
- chemical-sign agreement with the truth.

**Decision rules.**
1. **Arm B.** Final capture ≥ 90%: the optimizer reaches an in-class optimum
   at this budget, so the real shortfall is not an optimization failure on
   the deterministic part. Below 90%: optimization or the loss landscape limits
   the fit, and no real-data capture number reads as capacity.
2. **Arm A.** Its oracle capture (the truth itself) is the attainable level.
   - Fit capture more than 5 points above oracle capture: the fit absorbs noise at this budget.
   - Signal recovery ≥ 0.9: the planted response is recovered despite the noise.
   - Arm A's capture within 5 points of the real reference run (74.76% final):
     the real result is consistent with an in-class truth plus noise.
3. **Arm C.** Capture more than 5 points below arm B: the gate can detect a
   missing mechanism of this size. Within 5 points: Level 0 absorbs the
   mechanism, and the capacity gate cannot detect it.
4. **The gate.** If step 1 holds and arm A behaves as rule 2 describes, the 90%
   gate is retired for trial-mean targets. Its replacement is fixed in this
   document before any further real-data fit: real capture compared with the
   noise-ceiling expectation, with signal recovery on planted arms as the
   optimization check.

These are synthetic controls. They establish what the capacity number can and
cannot mean. They do not validate Level 0 biologically.
